Your options

What we review together, and what the evidence says

No single treatment suits every joint. These are the options an evaluation covers, described the way we'd describe them in the exam room.

Steroid injections

They can calm a flare for weeks to months. The relief is temporary, so we talk about where they fit and where they don't.

PRP (platelet-rich plasma)

Made from a small sample of your own blood. Research suggests it may reduce pain and improve function in some people with mild to moderate knee arthritis. It doesn't regrow cartilage, and it doesn't work for everyone.

Hyaluronic acid injections

The evidence is mixed. Some patients report relief, and we'll tell you whether your joint is a reasonable fit.

Surgery, when it's the right answer

Sometimes it is. If that's where your joint stands, you'll hear it plainly, along with what the path looks like.

Common questions

Am I a candidate for PRP?

It depends on the joint, how advanced the arthritis is, and your goals. That's what the evaluation answers. Some people are good candidates and some are better served by another option.

Does PRP regrow cartilage?

No. Current research does not show that PRP rebuilds cartilage. The studies that support it measure pain and function, and results vary from person to person.

How many injections will I need?

Standard and high-dose PRP are each done in a single visit. The PRP and hyaluronic acid gel series takes three visits. At your evaluation, Dr. Pradhan will tell you which option, if any, fits your joint.

Is it covered by insurance?

The evaluation is an office visit, and we verify your coverage before you come in. PRP itself is generally not covered, so our prices are posted on this page.

What if I've already been told I need a replacement?

Bring your imaging. We'll tell you whether we agree, and whether anything is worth trying first. If replacement is the right answer, you'll hear that too.
